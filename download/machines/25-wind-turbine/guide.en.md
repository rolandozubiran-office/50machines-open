## What it does

Hugh Piggott's design turns wind into 12–48 V power with hand tools.

This part of the tree: A battery, then a magnet, then a wheel-driven alternator. From here power can travel down a wire.

## Model

A Piggott wind turbine on a guyed tower: three carved wooden blades on a hub, the PM alternator, a yaw bearing, a tail that furls in high wind, a cable down to a battery bank and a dump load. Run: wind speed slider → rotor rpm → watts (≈ 500 W at 12 m/s, 2 m rotor per notes), tail furls at high wind.
